\begin{abstract}
Large language models read graphs only after the graph has been serialized into text,
and accuracy swings sharply with the serialization. Multi-agent debate is a natural
remedy: its premise is a \emph{verification asymmetry}, the assumption that checking a
claim is cheaper than producing one. That premise is normally asserted rather than
measured, because in the domains where debate is usually tested the intermediate steps
are not objectively checkable. Graph reasoning removes that excuse: an edge claim is
either in the encoding or it is not. We build a Proposer--Critic debate on GraphQA
(3~tasks $\times$ 3~encodings $\times$ 600~graphs, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) and measure the
asymmetry directly. It is absent. Pooled over 6{,}424 verdicts the Critic's
\textsc{revise} is statistically independent of whether the answer it judged was wrong
($\phi=+0.02$, $p=0.07$), and 4--26\% of the edges it cites as evidence do not exist in
the graph. Debate leaves mean accuracy unchanged (0.496 vs.\ 0.501) but is far from
inert per cell, moving accuracy by $-0.100$ to $+0.095$; it more than doubles the
encoding spread on \textsc{connected\_nodes} and manufactures a significant encoding
effect on \textsc{edge\_existence}, a task with none at baseline. We localize the cause
to the chain-of-thought scaffold rather than to the debate loop, and bound the loop's
remaining headroom at $+0.062$ with an oracle stopping rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A graph must be serialized before a language model can read it, and how it is serialized
matters. \citet{fatemi2024talk} show that zero-shot accuracy on elementary graph
questions varies sharply with the encoding, the task, and the graph, with no encoding
dominating. This is a practical liability wherever relational data meets an LLM: the same
model, the same graph, and a different wording give a different answer.

Multi-agent debate \citep{du2023debate} is an appealing remedy, and its appeal rests on
an assumption. \citet{irving2018debate} motivate debate by a \emph{verification
asymmetry}: checking a claim should be cheaper than producing one, so a critic of equal
capacity can police a proposer. In the domains where debate is usually evaluated --- math
word problems, open-ended QA --- that assumption cannot be tested, because the
intermediate steps are not objectively checkable. It is therefore unclear whether the
existing null results for debate \citep{huang2024selfcorrect, choi2025debateorvote} show
that debate does not work, or only that verification happened to be hard there.

Graph reasoning removes the ambiguity. An atomic claim is ``edge $(u,v)$ is present,''
and the encoding settles it by string lookup. If the verification asymmetry exists
anywhere, it exists here. We therefore ask:

\begin{quote}
Does a Proposer--Critic debate make graph reasoning robust to how the graph is written,
and does any gain come from the debate procedure rather than from extra compute?
\end{quote}

The answer is no on both counts, and the interesting part is why. Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item We reproduce encoding fragility over 5{,}400 instances with paired tests, and
      identify \textsc{edge\_existence} as a non-fragile control (\S\ref{sec:fragility}).
\item We show self-consistency at $10\times$ compute is indistinguishable from greedy in
      all nine cells, with a structural reason: terse answers leave no diverse reasoning
      paths to marginalize (\S\ref{sec:mv}).
\item We measure the assumed verification asymmetry and find it absent: the verdict
      carries $\approx$1 accuracy point of information, and in two cells it is
      \emph{anti}-correlated with correctness (\S\ref{sec:critic}).
\item We show debate's effect is not null but \emph{bidirectional} --- it redistributes
      accuracy across encodings, amplifying the fragility it targeted --- and attribute
      that to the chain-of-thought scaffold, not the loop
      (\S\ref{sec:debate}, \S\ref{sec:split}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Graph encodings.} \citet{fatemi2024talk} introduce GraphQA and establish that
LLM graph reasoning is encoding-dependent; on listing a node's neighbors, accuracy moves
from 19.8\% to 53.8\% by wording alone. GraphToken \citep{perozzi2024graphtoken} learns a
soft encoding and raises absolute performance, but the task-dependent sensitivity
remains. Both lines characterize or shift fragility; neither offers a mechanism that
removes it at inference time, which is the gap we probe.

\paragraph{Debate and its nulls.} \citet{du2023debate} report gains from multi-agent
debate; the gains are contested. \citet{huang2024selfcorrect} show LLMs cannot reliably
self-correct without external feedback, and \citet{choi2025debateorvote} attribute most
reported debate gains to the majority vote implicit in aggregation rather than to the
exchange of arguments, against the self-consistency baseline of
\citet{wang2023selfconsistency}. Our contribution is not another null but a
\emph{measurement of the mechanism} those nulls leave unexplained: we instrument the
critic's verdict against ground truth where verification is trivially checkable.

\paragraph{Chain of thought.} \citet{wei2022cot} show that eliciting intermediate steps
improves reasoning. Any debate protocol inherits this, since a proposer emitting a
verifiable trace is also doing CoT; prior evaluations rarely separate the two. We do, and
the separation carries the effect.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data.} We use the released GraphQA generators and encoders
\citep{fatemi2024talk} directly, so graph generation and encoder wording are byte-exact
to the original. Graphs are Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi with $n \sim \mathcal{U}\{5,\dots,19\}$
nodes and $p \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$; ground truth comes from NetworkX
\citep{hagberg2008networkx}. Structure is held fixed at ER because our variable is
encoding. We freeze three independent datasets of 200 graphs (seeds 7, 11, 13) and pool
them, giving $n=600$ per cell; because the three encodings are applied to the \emph{same}
graphs, all comparisons are paired.

\paragraph{Tasks and encodings.} Three GraphQA tasks --- \textsc{edge\_existence} (is
$(u,v)$ an edge?), \textsc{node\_degree} (degree of $u$), \textsc{connected\_nodes} (list
$u$'s neighbors) --- crossed with three encodings chosen to differ on \emph{both} axes of
node naming and edge phrasing: \textbf{adjacency} (integer nodes, parenthesized pairs),
\textbf{incident} (integer nodes, natural-language neighbor lists), and
\textbf{friendship} (named nodes, relational sentences). GraphQA's remaining encodings
are cover-story variants of friendship. This gives nine task\,$\times$\,encoding cells.

\paragraph{Model.} Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct \citep{qwen2025technical} in fp16, one model
serving both roles. The 3B size is forced by the 11\,GB GPUs available to us; this is a
deviation from the 7--8B models we planned, and we return to it in Limitations.

\paragraph{Conditions.} \textbf{Baseline}: one greedy zero-shot answer, GraphQA's
question verbatim plus a terse-format instruction, no CoT and no exemplars --- faithful to
the zero-shot row that produces the fragility result. \textbf{Majority vote}: $N=10$
samples of the same prompt at $T=0.7$ (top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20), resolved by the mode of
the parsed draws. \textbf{Debate}:
the Proposer answers with a numbered list of atomic edge claims and a final
\texttt{ANSWER:} line; the Critic is instructed to re-derive the answer from the edge list
and return \texttt{VERDICT: AGREE} or \texttt{VERDICT: REVISE} with each problem quoted
from the graph; the Proposer then revises. The loop runs to consensus, to no-progress (a
repeated answer), or to a budget of 10 responses, and the final answer is the last
Proposer answer. Debate is greedy throughout, so it shares the baseline's decoding.

\paragraph{Compute accounting.} We report cost per instance as \emph{model responses}
and \emph{total tokens} (prompt $+$ generated); generated-only would mislead, since a
baseline instance averages 374 total tokens but under 5 generated --- the encoded graph is
the whole cost. Baseline spends 1 response / 374 tokens, majority vote 10 / 3{,}659
($10.0\times$), debate 2.9 / 1{,}805 ($4.8\times$): debate is strictly \emph{cheaper}
than the vote it must beat.

\paragraph{Scoring and statistics.} Accuracy is whole-answer exact match against
NetworkX, with set equality for \textsc{connected\_nodes} and no partial credit, mirroring
GraphQA; a \texttt{parse\_ok} flag keeps parse failures visible rather than silently
scored wrong. Because encodings and conditions share graphs, significance is paired
throughout: McNemar for pairwise deltas (exact binomial below 25 discordant pairs) and
Cochran's $Q$ as the omnibus test. In total we score 5{,}400 baseline instances, 5{,}400
debate instances (13{,}926 responses), and 18{,}000 majority-vote samples.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Encoding fragility reproduces}
\label{sec:fragility}

The premise holds (Table~\ref{tab:main}, baseline column; Table~\ref{tab:fragility},
upper block). \textsc{node\_degree} spans 0.388 (adjacency) to 0.750 (incident), a gap of
0.362 with $Q=200.2$, $p=3\mathrm{e}{-44}$; the best-versus-worst McNemar is 258/41,
$p=8\mathrm{e}{-36}$. \textsc{connected\_nodes} spans 0.217 to 0.343
($Q=65.8$, $p=5\mathrm{e}{-15}$). The direction replicates
\citet{fatemi2024talk}: incident is best on both tasks.

\textsc{edge\_existence} is \emph{not} encoding-fragile: spread 0.013, $Q=0.52$,
$p=0.77$, and the best/worst labels differ across seeds --- the signature of no effect. We
treat it as a control, since an intervention has almost no fragility to remove there. It
is also the task the Critic's atomic check reduces to.

\subsection{Extra compute alone buys nothing}
\label{sec:mv}

Majority vote at $N=10$ is indistinguishable from a single greedy answer in all nine
cells (seed 7, $n=200$): every $|\Delta| \le 0.010$, only 1--8 discordant instances per
cell, and every McNemar $p \ge 0.625$. Voting returns greedy's exact correctness on
96--99\% of instances, and the fragility gaps are unchanged.

The reason is structural. Self-consistency draws its leverage from marginalizing over
\emph{diverse reasoning paths} \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}; our baseline emits a
direct terse answer, so there are no paths to marginalize, and a vote over samples of a
near-single-token answer converges to the argmax --- exactly greedy decoding. This is the
control debate needs: on these tasks $10\times$ the compute spent on more samples of the
same reasoning is worth nothing, so any debate gain must come from changing the
reasoning.

\subsection{Debate is not null --- it is bidirectional}
\label{sec:debate}

Mean accuracy over the nine cells is 0.496 for debate against 0.501 for the baseline, and
that gap is smaller than the seed-to-seed spread (per-seed deltas $+0.012$, $+0.002$,
$-0.029$), so on the mean the two are indistinguishable. Per cell
(Table~\ref{tab:main}) debate moves accuracy from $-0.100$ to $+0.095$ and five of nine
moves are significant --- three losses, two gains. The flat mean is cancellation, not
inertness.

The direction of that cancellation is the result. Debate \emph{amplifies} encoding
fragility (Table~\ref{tab:fragility}, Figure~\ref{fig:fragility}): the
\textsc{connected\_nodes} spread goes from 0.127 to 0.298, because debate lifts incident
by $+0.095$ while dropping adjacency by $-0.100$ and friendship by $-0.077$. The gains
land in the encoding that was already best, and unlike the mean this replicates in all
three seeds independently.

The sharpest form of this is \textsc{edge\_existence}. At baseline it is
encoding-insensitive ($Q=0.52$, $p=0.77$). Under debate the same task becomes
significantly encoding-sensitive ($Q=31.5$, $p=1.4\mathrm{e}{-07}$), with the spread
growing from 0.013 to 0.132. Debate does not merely fail to remove fragility; on a task
that had none, it manufactures it.

\subsection{The amplification is chain-of-thought, not debate}
\label{sec:split}

The baseline answers directly while the debate Proposer writes a claim trace, so
``debate vs.\ baseline'' conflates CoT with debate. Turn 1 of every transcript \emph{is} a
single-turn CoT answer at identical decoding, which separates the two with no extra runs
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).

The CoT step carries the movement, in both directions: $+0.077$ on
\textsc{edge\_existence}/incident against $-0.118$, $-0.090$ and $-0.052$ on three others
(four of nine significant, three of them losses). On the mean it is a net \emph{loss} of
0.019 --- the claim-trace scaffold is not free.

The loop step is smaller and better behaved: significant in two of nine cells, both
positive ($+0.060$ and $+0.055$), worth $+0.014$ on the mean, and it never reverses the
sign the scaffold set. The loop is therefore not literally inert --- it recovers part of
what the scaffold costs --- but it is a weak correction on top of a large format effect,
and it does not touch the spread. Fragility amplification is a property of the
\emph{reasoning format}, not of the verification procedure layered over it.

\paragraph{What the scaffold changes is the answer prior.} The claim trace does not make
the model reason better or worse --- it shifts which answer the model will commit to, away
from negative and empty answers. It says \emph{Yes} on \textsc{edge\_existence} 24\% of
the time as a bare baseline and 56\% with the scaffold (gold rate 51\%), and it answers
the empty list on \textsc{connected\_nodes} 22\% of the time as a baseline and
\textbf{0.9\%} with the scaffold (gold rate 12\%). Accuracy follows exactly:

on the 207 empty-gold \textsc{connected\_nodes} instances the baseline scores 0.990 and the
scaffold \textbf{0.077}, while on the other 1{,}593 the scaffold \emph{gains} (0.180 to
0.234); on \textsc{edge\_existence} the same signature, gold-No $0.963 \!\to\! 0.652$ and
gold-Yes $0.438 \!\to\! 0.780$. The scaffold is worth $+0.342$ where the answer is Yes and
$-0.311$ where it is No. Having written claims that name nodes, the model harvests those
names into its answer: one instance states ``There is no edge involving Robert'' and
answers ``James, John, Michael.'' A cell's apparent gain or loss is therefore largely a
fact about its gold prior. Fragility amplification is not an artefact of this --- on the
non-empty bucket the spread still widens, 0.141 to 0.258.

\subsection{The verification asymmetry is absent}
\label{sec:critic}

Debate's premise is that the Critic checks more reliably than the Proposer solves. We
test it by cross-tabulating every verdict against whether the Proposer answer \emph{that
verdict judged} was correct (Table~\ref{tab:critic}).

Pooled over 6{,}424 verdicts the Critic raises \textsc{revise} on 57.3\% of correct
answers and catches 59.6\% of wrong ones. Against a base rate of 0.535 wrong, a
\textsc{revise} moves $P(\text{wrong})$ only to 0.545: $\chi^2=3.36$, $p=0.067$,
$\phi=+0.023$, odds ratio 1.10. The verdict is worth about one accuracy point of
information and does not clear significance even at $N$ in the thousands. Three
observations sharpen this beyond ``the critic is weak.''

\textbf{The failure is calibration, not ignorance.} On the three
\textsc{edge\_existence} cells the Critic detects 95--96\% of wrong answers --- real
discrimination, $\phi$ up to $+0.27$ --- but simultaneously fires on 70--82\% of correct
ones. It knows something and cannot express it as a usable decision.

\textbf{Its flag rate is set backwards.} On the three \textsc{edge\_existence} cells the
Proposer is wrong about 30\% of the time and the Critic objects to roughly 80\% of its
answers; on the other six the Proposer is wrong 28--88\% of the time and the Critic objects
far less. It is most critical where the Proposer is most reliable. The high
\textsc{edge\_existence} detection figure is largely this: a near-constant \textsc{revise}
catches most errors because it catches most of everything. And on
\textsc{node\_degree}/adjacency the verdict is significantly \emph{anti}-correlated with
error ($\phi=-0.076$, $p=0.048$).

\textbf{Its evidence is frequently fabricated.} Told that the graph text is the only
source of truth and to quote the offending edge, the Critic cites pairs that do not exist
in the graph 4--26\% of the time, worst on \textsc{edge\_existence}/adjacency. On the
\textsc{edge\_existence} cells it usually cites no pair at all and writes prose (551 of
643 \textsc{revise}s on incident). The one sub-task the design assumed was easy --- is a
pair in a list --- is where grounding fails most.

Downstream, the Proposer changes its answer after only 15--44\% of \textsc{revise}s, and
the net effect of every revision in the study is $+74$ corrections over 3{,}759
critiques.

\subsection{How much headroom is left in the loop?}

One might object that the loop is fine and only its stopping rule is wrong. We bound that
with an oracle: per instance, the best answer any Proposer turn produced --- perfect
foresight over the turns that occurred. Pooled accuracy rises from 0.496 to 0.557
($+0.062$), and that ceiling is distributed exactly the wrong way: $+0.105$ to $+0.150$ on
the three \textsc{edge\_existence} cells (the non-fragile task) against $+0.012$ on
\textsc{node\_degree}/incident, leaving \textsc{connected\_nodes} spanning 0.160--0.467.
No stopping rule, not even a clairvoyant one, recovers the encoding gap here.

\section{Discussion}

Debate's premise did not survive contact with the setting we chose \emph{because} that
setting was maximally favorable to it. The Critic's job here reduces to
\textsc{edge\_existence}, a string lookup, on the task we independently measured to be
easiest and the only encoding-insensitive one. It still could not turn that into a verdict
better than noise, and it fabricated the evidence it was told to quote. This is stronger
than the existing nulls: \citet{huang2024selfcorrect} and \citet{choi2025debateorvote}
show debate fails where verification is hard, and one may always answer that a checkable
domain would differ. We built the checkable domain.

The second finding we did not expect. Interventions that raised mean accuracy raised the
encoding spread with it, and the oracle ceiling redistributes the same way. If this
generalizes, robustness to serialization is not something better inference-time reasoning
delivers for free --- it may be in tension with it, because a scaffold is itself a format
that some encodings suit better than others.

A methodological note that cost most of the project's effort: our first Proposer prompt
contained a bug that presented as a finding. Its answer hint requested ``a comma-separated
list of node ids'' for every encoding, but friendship labels nodes with names; the model
complied, emitted integers, and 64 of 600 answers became unparseable --- reading as
evidence that friendship is hard to reason over, when it was a label-space mismatch the
baseline never had. A later revision meant to strengthen all three roles instead drove the
Proposer's capitulation rate from 0.63 to 0.96, costing 0.046 mean accuracy. Negative
results about prompt-mediated methods are only as trustworthy as the prompt forensics
behind them, and these failures are invisible in accuracy tables.

\section{Conclusion}

In a domain built so that atomic verification is trivial, a Proposer--Critic debate
produced verdicts statistically independent of correctness, evidence fabricated up to a
quarter of the time, no mean gain over greedy, and double the encoding spread on one task.
The mechanism debate assumes is absent at this scale; what movement exists comes from the
chain-of-thought scaffold. The next experiment is asymmetric --- a 3B Proposer with a much
larger Critic. If verification is still near chance there, the premise is in trouble.

\section*{Limitations}

\textbf{Scale.} Every result is for a single 3B model in both roles, forced by an 11\,GB
VRAM ceiling. Verification may become cheap relative to generation only above some
capability threshold: our result is that it is not cheap at 3B, not that it is never
cheap. The asymmetric-critic experiment is the direct test, and we did not run it.

\textbf{Critic granularity.} Our Critic is holistic --- it re-derives the answer and
returns one verdict. Per-claim verification, one binary question per atomic claim
aggregated in code, was not run, and the \textsc{edge\_existence} cells suggest it might
behave differently: the Critic discriminates there (detection 0.95, $\phi=+0.27$) and only
fails to turn that into a verdict.

\textbf{Comparison budget.} Majority vote was run on one of three seeds ($n=200$ per
cell), a weaker claim than the pooled $n=600$ used elsewhere. Debate and majority vote are
compared transitively through the shared baseline rather than head to head; since debate
spends less than the vote (2.9 vs.\ 10 responses) and neither beats the baseline, no
budget-matching scheme changes the conclusion.

\textbf{Scope.} One model family, one generator (ER), one size range, three encodings.
\textsc{friendship} confounds non-integer labels with a social cover story that may invite
transitive closure; an alphabetic-label encoding with neutral phrasing would separate
them. Finally, the debate arm is one frozen prompt: given that a prompt bug once
masqueraded as a finding (\S5), we treat per-cell deltas as properties of \emph{this}
wording and the critic-quality measurement --- which does not depend on the answer format
--- as the more portable result.

\section{Prompt texts}
\label{sec:appendix}

\paragraph{Baseline (zero-shot).} A per-task terse-format instruction, then GraphQA's
question block verbatim (encoded graph, \texttt{Q: \dots}, \texttt{A: }). For example,
\textsc{node\_degree} uses \emph{``Answer with a single integer (the degree) and nothing
else.''}

\paragraph{Proposer.} \emph{``Answer the question below using the graph. Build the answer
as a numbered list of atomic claims --- each claim one verifiable fact about a single edge
(that it exists, or that no further edge involves the node) --- then give the final answer.
Number your claims 1., 2., 3., and so on, one claim per line. Then write a final line
that begins with \texttt{ANSWER:} followed by \dots. Write nothing after that line.''}
The answer-format tail is deliberately label-space free (``written exactly as the graph
writes them'') so one wording covers all three encodings.

\paragraph{Critic.} The Critic is told the graph text is the only source of truth, asked
to independently enumerate the relevant edges from the list before comparing, to check in
both directions (missed edges and invented edges), and to answer in exactly
\texttt{VERDICT: AGREE} or \texttt{VERDICT: REVISE} followed by problems, each quoting an
edge from the graph's edge list.

\section{Reproducibility}

All numbers regenerate on CPU from committed run outputs. Raw model output is persisted
per attempt, with full debate transcripts in trace sidecars, so scoring changes are
re-applied without re-running the GPU.
